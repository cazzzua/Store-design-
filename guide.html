Check these two things before you run ads

Your site is on the Wix Free plan right now. Wix only takes payments on a paid plan that includes eCommerce, and a custom domain (like pestillence.com) also needs a paid plan. Until you upgrade, people can browse but can't pay.

The coded landing page (index.html) sits inside an HTML embed, which is a box that can't talk to the Wix cart. Its buttons open your real product pages, and shoppers buy from there. If you want an "Add to Cart" button on the landing page itself, use the Wix Stores elements described in step 2b, Option 1.

01

How the landing page connects to your real store

Your landing page isn't a separate website. It's one more page on the same Wix site as your store, just like your home page or your product pages. You could put it at an address like cazzzua.wixstudio.com/my-site-2/gothic-drop, and later at yourdomain.com/gothic-drop once you connect a domain.

Because it lives on the same site, you don't need an integration, an app or any code to "connect" it. It already has access to everything Wix Stores has: the same 31 products, the same prices, sizes and stock, the same cart, and the same checkout.

The connection is links and store elements. A "Shop now" button links to one of your collection pages. A product card links to that product's page. A Wix Stores product gallery placed on the landing page pulls products straight from your catalogue, with working Add to Cart buttons.

So a customer can start buying on the landing page: add a tee to the cart, open the cart and go to checkout. If they'd rather look around first, one link takes them into the full store, and whatever is already in their cart stays there.

Step-by-step: setting up buttons and product links on the landing page

Your store addresses follow this pattern. Wix uses /category/ for collections and /product-page/ for products, not /shop/ or /product/.

PageAddress
All products (31)…/my-site-2/category/all-products
T-shirts (19)…/my-site-2/category/t-shirts
Hoodies (12)…/my-site-2/category/hoodies
One product…/my-site-2/product-page/last-rites-hooded-reaper-tee

The product address comes straight from your store. The three collection addresses follow the usual Wix pattern built from your category names. Open each one in a browser once to make sure it loads before you use it.

a) "Shop now" and "View the collection" buttons (hero and final call to action)

  1. Open your site in Wix Studio and go to the landing page (Pages panel, then the page name).
  2. Click the button once to select it. A blue frame and a small toolbar appear.
  3. Click the link icon (it looks like a chain) in the toolbar. You can also find Link in the Inspector panel on the right.
  4. Choose Web address and paste the collection address. Set it to open in the same tab, so the shopper stays in your store.
  5. To link to a regular page such as your home page, choose Page instead and pick it from the list.
  6. Double-click the button to edit its text. Short and direct works best: "Shop now", "Shop the drop" or "View the full collection".
  7. Click Preview, then the button, and make sure the right page opens.

b) Product buttons on the landing page ("Add to Cart", "Buy Now", "View")

Option 1: Wix Stores product elements on the page

  1. Click Add Elements (+) on the left and open Store.
  2. Drag a product gallery (grid or slider) onto the landing page. For one featured tee, you can add a single product widget or an Add to Cart button element and connect it to that product.
  3. Pick which products appear. A gallery shows one category, so the simplest way is to create a category in your dashboard called something like "Gothic Drop", add the same three tees as the landing page (Last Rites, Horned Madonna, Sainted Scream), and point the gallery at it in the gallery's settings. Leave it out of your menu so it only appears here.
  4. In the gallery's settings, turn on the Add to Cart button. Every tee comes in S to 5XL, so also turn on product options (or quick view) so shoppers pick a size before adding.
  5. Set what happens after "Add to Cart" so the side cart (mini cart) opens. The shopper sees their item, stays on the landing page, and can tap Checkout straight away.
  6. Change the button text if you like. "Add to Cart" is the clearest. "Buy Now" also works for a single featured product.

Wix renames panels now and then, so labels in your editor may differ slightly from the ones here. The order of steps stays the same.

About checkout: there's no popup checkout. When the shopper taps Checkout in the side cart, Wix opens its own checkout page on your site, with the same address bar and the same store. It only appears after they've decided to pay.

Option 2: regular buttons that link to product pages

  1. Click Add Elements (+), then Button, and drag one under your product image.
  2. Click the link icon, choose Web address, and paste the product's address.
  3. Use a label that says what happens next: "View", "See details" or "Buy now". The product page is where they pick a size and add to cart.

c) "See more products" and "Visit full store" buttons

  1. Put it after the main products, near the final "Shop now". By then the shopper has seen the drop, so the link reads as a next step, not a distraction.
  2. Make it a text link or an outlined (secondary) button, smaller than your main button.
  3. Link it to all products, or to the collection that matches the ad. Someone who came for a hoodie ad should land on hoodies.
  4. Write it like a person would say it: "Explore the full collection", "See more gothic pieces" or "Explore all 31 pieces".

03

Customer buying flow (from ad to checkout)

Say Maya in Manchester is scrolling Instagram late at night.

  1. She sees your Last Rites reaper tee in a Reel and taps "Shop now".
  2. She lands on your landing page at yourdomain.com/gothic-drop. It's black, the tee from the ad is right at the top, and the strip says shipping is free.
  3. She scrolls down. There are only three tees, each with a big photo of the front and back, the price and the sizes, so there's no wall of products to get lost in.
  4. She taps Buy now · $35.90 under Last Rites, picks size M on the product page and taps Add to Cart. With a Wix Stores gallery on the landing page, she can add to cart without leaving it.
  5. The side cart slides open with her tee in it. She taps Checkout.
  6. She pays on your normal Wix checkout with whichever methods you've switched on, such as card or PayPal.
  7. At any point she could have tapped "Explore the full collection" to look at hoodies first. Her cart would still be waiting at checkout.
04

Letting customers visit the full store from the landing page

Where to put it

  • One quiet link in the header, top right, such as "Full store →". People who want to browse will look there.
  • One small row under the final "Shop now", for example: "Want to see more? Explore all 31 pieces · Hoodies · All T-shirts".
  • Leave it out of the hero. The first screen has one job: getting people to the products.

What to call it

  • "Explore the full collection" or "See more gothic pieces". These sound like an invitation.
  • Avoid "Leave", "Home" or "Back to store". They make the landing page feel like a detour.

Header or footer?

Keep a minimal header on this page: your logo plus one "Full store" link. Hide the full site menu on this page if you can (in Wix Studio you can hide the header elements on a single page, or give the page its own simple header). A footer link is fine too, but by the time someone reaches your footer, the final call to action has already done the work.

How to keep exits under control

  • One main action, repeated: "Shop now" in the hero, product buttons in the middle, and "Shop now" again at the end. All of them lead into buying.
  • Two or three ways out at most: the header link and the "see more" row. No social icons, blog links or About page links here.
  • Make exits look quieter than buying. Your main button gets the blue outline. Exit links stay grey and small.
  • Match the ad. A hoodie ad should point the "see more" link to hoodies, not to everything.
05

Optional: tracking and orders

  • Every sale shows up in your Wix dashboard under Orders, whether the customer started on the landing page, a product page or the home page. Printful or Printify get it the same way they do now.
  • Add UTM tags to your ad links so you can see which ad brought people in. For example: yourdomain.com/gothic-drop?utm_source=instagram&utm_campaign=gothic_drop. Use utm_source=tiktok for TikTok ads. Wix Analytics groups visits by these tags.
  • You don't need separate systems. The landing page and the store are one Wix site with one cart, one checkout and one list of orders.
